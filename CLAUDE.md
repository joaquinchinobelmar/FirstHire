Summary
FirstHire helps small North East firms turn a real business problem into a paid first
opportunity for a local student or graduate. The firm describes the problem, Claude
shapes it into a fair role, and students discover it through a simple swipe feed. A mentor
from a bigger company supports the work.
For firms: a short paid project, part-time role, internship or first job, with the cost and
support needed made clear before they commit.
For students: one Passport, local career paths and relevant opportunities that fit
around existing part-time work.
For mentors and larger employers: early sight of local talent, in return for a small,
structured amount of support.
For universities: more local paid experience for students, with less manual work for
careers teams.
The firm pays. Students always use it free.
The problem and who has it
Our first customer is the owner of a 5 to 49-person North East firm that has work piling
up but has never hired a graduate. Around 15,000 firms in the wider region fall in this size
band. That is a rounded estimate from official size shares, and it includes Tees Valley.
The firm's pain: many small firms don't see graduates as a natural hire, and they worry
about experience and supervision. This research is older, so we must test it locally.
The student's pain: 65% of full-time UK undergraduates work during term, and that
pay covers about 48% of typical monthly spending among working students.
Students may need paid experience, not unpaid internships.
The region's pain: 85% of students who came from outside the region leave after
graduating, according to 2023 analysis.
Why now: new apprenticeship and hiring support exists, but many SMEs find the
support landscape fragmented. The North East Growth Hub identified that problem
in May 2026.

What Claude does
1. Turns rough notes into a realistic scope, with tasks and outcomes.
2. Identifies whether the work suits a paid project, part-time role, internship or first job.
3. Explains the skills needed and removes unnecessary requirements, such as a degree
when it isn't needed.
4. Maps a student's course and part-time experience to the role and a possible local
career path, with its reasoning shown.
Code, not Claude, calculates pay and checks fixed rules.
Trust, fairness and legal safeguards
Every opportunity must be paid, every applicant must get a fair first review, and people
must make the final decisions. We need legal review before launch, but these rules shape
the prototype now.
Risk Safeguard
Unpaid or underpaid work Don't publish unpaid opportunities. If an intern
counts as a worker, they are normally due the
National Minimum Wage, whatever the role is
called.
Freelance gigs International students on a Student visa can't be
self-employed or do freelance work. Paid projects
therefore start as employment, not gigs.
Too many hours Student visa holders on degree courses are usually
limited to 20 hours a week in term. The app collects
availability, and the student confirms their own
limit.
Biased selection Hide name, photo and university in the first review.
AI doesn't rank applicants.
Weak intern output Each role has a defined scope, a realistic plan and
regular check-ins with the firm and mentor.
Mentor conflict of interest Mentors can't approach the intern about another
job during the placement. They also keep firm
information confidential.

Business model
The firm is the main customer, at a provisional £49 a month with a three-month
minimum term. At 100 paying firms, that's £4,900 of monthly revenue before costs. Stage
4 will calculate cost to serve, break-even and profit.
Firms also pay the student directly. For example, a 12-week role at 20 hours a week and
the April 2026 National Living Wage of £12.71 costs £3,050.40 in wages before employer
costs. Actual wage rates depend on age and the role.
Risk Safeguard
AI mistakes The firm reviews each draft, sees its reasoning and
can edit everything. FirstHire doesn't give legal
advice.
Outreach privacy Named directors are personal data. Email
marketing rules also treat sole traders and some
partnerships as individuals, so we'd need clear
contact rules.
People left out Build for mobile first, keep applications short and
show hours and travel clearly.

First 100 customers
Start with 5 to 49-person firms in professional services, manufacturing, construction
and care, focusing first on Newcastle and Gateshead. These sectors are more likely to
offer useful career paths than hospitality, even though hospitality has the most firms in
this size band.
List: use Companies House to find active companies by SIC code and registered
office location. Companies House doesn't show every local business, so add street
visits and referrals.
Warm routes: ask universities, the Chamber of Commerce, the Growth Hub and
mentors' employers to introduce suitable firms.
First offer: a free 30-minute session that turns one business problem into a role plan.
The firm pays only if it chooses to go ahead.

What we build today
Build one working loop with three screens: firm, student and applicant review. We
should pitch "swipe to discover local opportunities", not "Tinder for jobs".
1. Firm screen: paste rough notes from a synthetic Gateshead firm. Claude returns the
opportunity type, role, 12-week plan, skills and reasoning. Code calculates the wage
cost.
2. Student screen: load a synthetic Passport for a student with a café job. Swipe to the
role, see why it fits and answer one role-specific question.
3. Review screen: show anonymised applicants with evidence, not an AI ranking. The
firm selects one and a synthetic mentor is assigned.
Cut in this order if time runs short: voice input, real swipe animation, career-path detail,
mentor assignment, then the applicant view. Never cut the firm journey.

Risks and what to test
The biggest risk is that firms won't pay while free university schemes and job boards
exist. The plan works only if a firm values the scoping, trust and support, not just the
advert.
